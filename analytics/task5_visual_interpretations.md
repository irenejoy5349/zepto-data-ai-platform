# Task 5 – Visual Exploratory Analysis

## Chart 1 – Survival Rate by Sex

The survival rate for female passengers was 74.04%, compared with 18.89% for male passengers. This indicates a strong difference in survival outcomes between the two groups. Sex appears to be an important variable associated with survival.

## Chart 2 – Survival Rate by Passenger Class

Passengers in first class had a survival rate of 62.62%, while second-class passengers had 47.28% and third-class passengers had 24.24%. Survival decreased as passenger class moved from first to third class, suggesting that passenger class was strongly associated with survival.

## Chart 3 – Survival Rate by Sex and Passenger Class

Combining sex and passenger class reveals a stronger survival pattern than either variable alone. Female passengers generally had higher survival rates within each class, while third-class passengers had lower survival rates. This suggests that the interaction between sex and class is important for understanding survival outcomes.

## Chart 4 – Fare Distribution by Survival Status

The median fare for survivors was 26.00, compared with 10.50 for passengers who did not survive. The higher median fare among survivors suggests that fare level was associated with survival. The distribution also contains high-fare outliers.

## Chart 5 – Age Distribution by Survival Status

The median age among survivors was 28.00, while the median age among non-survivors was 28.00. The age distributions overlap substantially, so age alone does not provide as clear a separation as sex or passenger class. Age may still contribute useful information when combined with other variables in a predictive model.
